AI‑assisted thinking vs AI‑dependent thinking

4 min read

AI can now produce a surprising amount of the work that used to take hours.

It can turn rough notes into a document, write a PRD, analyse data, compare options, write and review code or research a topic and structure the findings.

But producing something is different from understanding it.

As AI takes on more of the work, the thinking behind it becomes even more important.

I have found it useful to distinguish between AI-assisted thinking and AI-dependent thinking.

The difference is simple.

AI-dependent thinking is when I give AI a problem and mostly accept the thinking it produces.

AI-assisted thinking is when I use AI to make my own thinking better.

The output can be very different.

How I work with AI

Take product work as an example.

Good product work starts with understanding the problem, not writing the PRD.

  • What are we actually trying to solve?
  • Who is affected?
  • What do we know?
  • What are we assuming?
  • What would happen if we did nothing?
  • What are the simplest ways we could approach it?

These questions help establish the problem and shape the decisions that follow. AI can help explore them, challenge assumptions and compare approaches. But someone still needs to make sense of the answers and decide what to do.

In my own workflow, I use AI throughout this process. Sometimes I ask it to challenge an idea. Sometimes I ask it to find gaps or compare two approaches. Sometimes I use it to structure something I have already decided.

And sometimes I hand over the mechanical work completely.

That last distinction matters.

I am happy for AI to turn an agreed product decision into a detailed document.

But I am much less comfortable letting it make the decision simply because it has produced a convincing-looking document.

The goal is not to do everything ourselves. It is to know which parts of the work we can delegate and which decisions we need to understand and own.

The test I use

Before accepting an important AI output, I want to be able to answer:

Before you accept an AI output
  • Do I understand why this is the right approach?
  • What assumptions is it based on?
  • What alternatives did we consider?
  • Where could this approach break?
  • Could I explain the decision without showing someone the AI conversation?

If I can't answer those questions, I probably don't understand the output well enough yet.

That doesn't mean I need to do all the work myself.

Quite the opposite.

The point of AI-assisted thinking is that AI can do much more of the work while I stay responsible for the parts that require judgment.

That is a very different model from simply asking AI to think for me.

And this applies to much more than product work. Whether we are analysing data, developing a strategy, writing a research paper or solving a business problem, the same principle holds.

The bigger shift

I think this distinction will become increasingly important as AI gets better.

It is easy to look at an AI-generated output and conclude that someone is doing sophisticated work.

But AI can make a weak thought process look very polished. It can also make a strong thinker dramatically more capable.

Those two things can look almost identical from the outside.

So the question worth asking is:

Is AI making us more capable, or is it simply making a half-cooked dish look better?

Try this

The next time you use AI for something important, pause and ask yourself: Has this helped me think better, or has it just done the thinking for me?
