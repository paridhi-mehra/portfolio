AI‑assisted thinking vs AI‑dependent thinking

2 min read

AI can now produce a surprising amount of work in minutes. It can turn rough notes into a document, write a PRD, analyse data, compare options, write and review code, research a topic and much more.

But producing something is different from understanding it.

As AI takes on more of the work, I have found it useful to distinguish between two ways of working with it.

  • AI-dependent thinking - when I give AI a problem and mostly accept the thinking it produces.
  • AI-assisted thinking - when I use AI as a thinking partner to challenge and improve my own thinking.

The output can look very similar. The difference is in how we get there.

Where the thinking happens

Take product work as an example.

Good product work starts with understanding the problem, not writing the PRD.

  • What are we actually trying to solve?
  • Who is affected?
  • What do we know and what are we assuming?
  • What would happen if we did nothing?
  • What are the simplest ways to approach it?

AI can help explore these questions, challenge assumptions and compare approaches. But someone still needs to make sense of the answers and decide what to do.

In my own workflow, I use AI at different stages. I ask it to challenge an idea, find gaps, compare approaches or structure something I have already decided. I also hand over mechanical work completely when the direction is clear.

For example, I am happy to let AI turn an agreed product decision into a detailed document. But I do not want it making the decision simply because it can produce a convincing-looking document.

The goal is not to do everything ourselves. It is to know what we can delegate and which decisions we need to understand and own.

Before accepting the output

A few questions I try to answer before accepting an important AI output:

Checklist
  • Do I understand why this is the right approach?
  • What assumptions is it based on?
  • What alternatives did we consider?
  • Where could this approach break?
  • Could I explain the decision without showing someone the AI conversation?

If I cannot answer these questions, I probably do not understand the output well enough yet.

And this applies to much more than product work. Whether we are analysing data, developing a strategy, writing a research paper or solving a business problem, the same principle holds.

Looking beyond the output

It is easy to look at an AI-generated output and conclude that someone is doing sophisticated work.

AI can make a weak thought process look very polished. It can also make a strong thinker dramatically more capable.

Those two things can look almost identical from the outside.

So the question worth asking is:

Is AI making us more capable, or is it simply making a half-cooked dish look better?

Try this

The next time you use AI for something important, pause and ask yourself: Has this helped me think better, or has it just done the thinking for me?
